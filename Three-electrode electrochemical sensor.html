Three-electrode electrochemical sensor

A potentiostat sweeps the potential of the working electrode and records the current that flows. Here the analyte is ferricyanide, Fe(CN)₆³⁻, which gains one electron at the surface of the working electrode. Press Run scan and change the concentration to see the sensor respond.

Cyclic voltammogram

The scan starts at +0.70 V, sweeps down to −0.10 V and back. The cathodic peak is where Fe(CN)₆³⁻ is reduced faster than fresh analyte can diffuse in.

Calibration curve

Each finished scan adds a point. Peak current rises in proportion to concentration (Randles–Ševčík), so the slope is the sensitivity. Keep the scan rate fixed while you collect points.

What each electrode does

Working electrode (WE)

The sensing surface. The analyte reacts here, and the current from that reaction is the signal.

Reference electrode (RE)

Ag/AgCl holds a fixed, stable potential. The potentiostat measures the WE against it and draws almost no current through it.

Counter electrode (CE)

A platinum wire that completes the circuit. It carries the current, so the opposite reaction happens here and the WE and RE stay undisturbed.
